  CAVEAT, ATTACHED: CLASS SATURATION — AND WHAT IT IMPLIES THE GAP IS NOT.
    The agent submitted the class maximum EXACTLY in 80 of 80 s0 runs and
    40 of 40 s3 runs, verified by SUPPORT and not only by score. So both tiers
    price the SAME STATISTIC, and their p-values are near-identical where both
    exist: equal on 58 of 60 s0 runs and 19 of 21 s3 runs, mean difference
    +0.0002 and +0.0005. A power difference therefore CANNOT come from the
    nulls or from the statistic, and the comparison carries no information
    about which tier is the more powerful test.
    What the measured gap IS: the decomposition above shows it comes entirely
    from the runs the replay tier DECLINES TO CERTIFY because the agent
    changed a declared rule. On the runs both tiers price, replay is level
    with the class tier or marginally ahead. So this is the MEASURED PRICE OF
    THE BRACKET — the power the replay tier forgoes by refusing a run whose
    rule changed — and not a statistical deficit. It is consistent with 7.0's
    finding that the two tiers are indistinguishable at matched actual size,
    which held on runs both tiers priced.
    Read as a consistency check on the machinery plus a measurement of the
    bracket's cost; NOT as evidence about tier power.
