# E19: does P4 survive when features are not exchangeable?

Committed before running. Code: `experiments/e19_heterogeneous.py`. E19(c),
the width sweep, is registered separately in `prereg/E19c.md` and has run
(SCOPE.md §19).

## Question

THEORY.md P4's lemma assumes a symmetric, nondecreasing pair statistic, which
holds when base columns are exchangeable: equal volatilities and one common
correlation. Real wide datasets are not like that. With unequal correlations
the pair statistic's denominator depends on which pair is formed, so the
lemma's hypothesis fails and the search can prefer a weaker but less
correlated partner.

Two arms:

- **(a)** Does the lemma's *conclusion* still hold, and do the corrections
  stay calibrated, when correlations are unequal?
- **(b)** P6's open case. Under equicorrelation the population correlations
  with the winner tie exactly, so the neighbor rule's anchor is decided by
  estimation noise, which standard bootstrap arguments do not cover. Unequal
  correlations separate them. How much does that change the anchor's
  stability, and does the recursive bootstrap stay calibrated?

The generator's `heterogeneous=True` gives a single-factor structure,
`Sigma = outer(l, l)` with a unit diagonal, loadings drawn uniformly on
√ρ ± 0.15. Feature volatilities stay equal; no generator change is made for
this experiment.

## Limit prediction, computed before running

Winner-anchored search at depth 2, ρ=0.3, 40,000 draws per cell, the null
quantile taken per structure. `M_P` is the process maximum and `M_C` the
frozen-menu maximum on the same replicate.

| structure | K | naive type-I | replicates with M_P ≥ M_C |
|---|---|---|---|
| equicorrelated (E19(c)'s control) | 20 | 9.09% ± 0.14 | 100% |
| heterogeneous correlation | 20 | 9.14% ± 0.14 | 99.96% |
| equicorrelated (E19(c)'s control) | 80 | 13.51% ± 0.17 | 100% |
| heterogeneous correlation | 80 | 14.38% ± 0.18 | 99.97% |

So the limit says the lemma's conclusion survives almost everywhere even
though its hypothesis fails, and that unequal correlation changes the
inflation itself by about a point at K=80 and not at all at K=20.

**What this experiment cannot do.** At n=500 a rate near 9% has a standard
error of 1.3 points, so a one-point difference between structures is far
below what it can detect. E19 therefore does not test whether heterogeneity
changes the inflation. It tests whether the conclusion and the corrections
survive it.

## Design input for arm (b), measured before running

Over 40 draws at K=20, ρ=0.3, comparing the neighbor rule under the two
structures: the anchor is the highest-loading feature on 28% of draws under
heterogeneity against 5% under equicorrelation (5% is chance), replicate
anchor stability rises from 0.08 to 0.19, and mean κ is 0.41 under both.

Heterogeneity therefore only **partly** separates the correlations. The
loadings span 0.40–0.70, so population correlations with the winner differ
modestly and sample noise at T=500 still decides the ordering on most
replicates. Arm (b) measures how much separation this buys and whether the
recursive bootstrap stays calibrated as it increases. It does not put the
neighbor rule in the regular case, and no claim that it does will be made.

## Design

| label | searcher | K | structure | draws |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `winner_het_K20` | `WinnerAnchor(max_features=2)` | 20 | heterogeneous | 500 |
| `winner_het_K80` | `WinnerAnchor(max_features=2)` | 80 | heterogeneous | 500 |
| `neighbor_het_K20` | `NeighborAdaptive(max_features=2)` | 20 | heterogeneous | 500 |
| `neighbor_equi_K20` | `NeighborAdaptive(max_features=2)` | 20 | equicorrelated | 500 |

- ρ=0.3, M=50, T=500, s=0, sigma=1, B=1500.
- Seeds 70000–70499, the same block E19(c) used, so each draw index has an
  equicorrelated counterpart already measured at the same K. The data still
  differ between structures; the seeds are shared, not the draws.
- Three nulls per draw, on common resampled indices (one block length from
  `select_block_length` on the demeaned base columns, bootstrap seed = the
  draw's seed): naive, recursive, and full-class over
  `full_class_matrix(base, 2)` (210 members at K=20, 3,240 at K=80).
- Recorded per draw: the three p-values; the count of replicates with
  M_P ≥ M_C and the mean gap; κ; the anchor's replicate stability for the
  neighbor cells (300 replicates, an independent index stream); `sr_sel`;
  the block length. Git state is captured at launch.
- Arm (a)'s equicorrelated controls are E19(c)'s cells at the same K and
  seeds (SCOPE.md §19) and are not rerun.

## Decision rules

Thresholds at n=500: the one-sided exact binomial against 5% rejects at
34/500 (6.8%) and has 80% power at a true rate of 7.8%; the Wilson lower
bound exceeds 5% at 35/500. The dominance share is pooled over
500 × 1500 = 750,000 replicates, where a true share of 0.9995 carries a
standard error of 2.6×10⁻⁵.

1. **P4's conclusion (primary, arm (a)).** In each winner cell, the pooled
   share of replicates with M_P ≥ M_C is at least 0.99.
   - Holds: the note states that winner anchoring's dominance survives
     unequal correlation, with the lemma covering the exchangeable case and
     this measurement covering the tested departure from it.
   - Fails: the note restricts P4's conclusion to exchangeable features, and
     the gate's reasoning about adaptive menus is revisited.
2. **Naive inflation (arm (a)).** Naive type-I is above 5% by the binomial
   test in both winner cells. Predicted, from the limit: 9.1% at K=20 (power
   0.97) and 14.4% at K=80 (power 1.00).
3. **Corrections (arms (a) and (b)).** Recursive and full-class type-I are
   not significantly above 5% in any of the four cells: Wilson lower bound at
   most 5%, that is at most 34 rejections of 500.
   - Fails in a winner cell: the recursive bootstrap's validity does not
     extend to unequal correlations, which is a limit on the gate itself.
   - Fails in a neighbor cell: P6's open case is a real failure, not just an
     unproven one.
4. **Anchor separation (arm (b)).** Mean replicate anchor stability is higher
   under heterogeneous correlation than under equicorrelation, by a
   one-sided paired sign test over the 500 shared seeds at p < 0.05.
   Predicted from the design input above: about 0.19 against 0.08.

## Secondary, reported but not deciding

1. Per-cell type-I with Wilson intervals and KS tests, for all three nulls.
2. The share of draws with naive p ≤ recursive p; P4's conclusion predicts
   all or nearly all in the winner cells.
3. Mean κ per cell, and the share of draws whose neighbor anchor is the
   highest-loading feature.
4. Naive type-I against E19(c)'s equicorrelated cells at the same K and
   seeds, reported with the caveat in "What this experiment cannot do": a
   one-point difference is not detectable here.
5. The mean gap M_P − M_C, and the largest violation of dominance.
