# E17b: does naive inflation grade with the anchor's rank, or step near the top?

Committed before running. Code: `experiments/e17b_anchor_rank.py`.

## Question

E17 (SCOPE.md §16) established that naive type-I rises with coupling across
anchor rules. Its exploratory breakdown suggested the inflation sits on
anchors among the top few features, but conditioning on the rank a rule
happened to pick selects draws. This experiment fixes the anchor's rank by
design. The answer decides how the winner-chasing audit summarizes κ from a
transcript: as a count of anchors at the top ranks, or as a graded function
of rank.

## Limit prediction, computed before running

Take THEORY.md P4's setting: equal-variance, equicorrelated base columns
with correlation ω, the Gaussian limit, depth 2, and the pair statistic
`g(x, y) = (x + y) / √(2 + 2ω)`. The search anchored on the rank-k feature
submits

    M(1) = max(Z_(1), g(Z_(1), Z_(2)))
    M(k) = max(Z_(1), g(Z_(k), Z_(1)))   for k ≥ 2

because the best partner of the winner is the runner-up, and the best
partner of any other feature is the winner. So ranks 1 and 2 submit the same
value on every draw, and M(k) is nonincreasing in k. The naive null freezes
the anchor's index, and in the limit that null is the same whichever fixed
index it is.

The anchor-at-rank-2 null also satisfies P4's lemma: its process maximum is
at least the frozen-menu maximum on every replicate, so the naive p-value is
at most the recursive one, as for the winner.

Monte Carlo of the limit, 2×10⁶ draws, ω = 0.302 (the mean correlation of
base columns measured on 30 draws of this configuration), standard error
about 0.02 points:

| anchor rank k | 1 | 2 | 3 | 5 | 10 | 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| limit naive type-I | 9.1% | 9.1% | 6.6% | 4.9% | 4.3% | 4.3% |

At finite T the winner rule has measured lower: 7.0% in E17, and 8.5% pooled
over e15, E16 and E17.

## Design

- `RankAnchor(rank=k, max_features=2)` (`searchers/dose_response.py`). Round 2
  expands around the feature whose single-feature Sharpe ranks k-th; a replay
  re-ranks on the resampled data. k ∈ {1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20}. Rank 1 is the
  winner rule and rank 20 the loser rule.
- E17's configuration: K=20, M=50, T=500, ρ=0.3 equicorrelated, s=0, sigma=1,
  B=1500.
- n=1,000 draws per rank, seeds 50000–50999 (used by no earlier experiment),
  paired across ranks.
- Naive and recursive nulls on common resampled indices: one block length
  (`select_block_length` on the demeaned base columns) and bootstrap
  seed = the draw's seed. No full-class null: E17 measured it at depth 2, and
  it does not bear on this question.
- Recorded per draw: both p-values, `sr_sel`, the anchor, κ and the block
  length. Git state is captured at launch.

## Decision rule

Two exact McNemar tests on the paired naive rejection decisions (a draw
rejects when its naive p-value is below 0.05), each one-sided in the
direction of fewer rejections for the lower-ranked anchor, at p < 0.05:

- **U:** rank 3 against rank 2. Does rank 3 fall below the top?
- **L:** rank 3 against rank 10. Does rank 3 stay above the bottom?

Exactly one outcome:

- **Graded** (U and L both significant). An intermediate anchor gives
  intermediate inflation, so the audit summarizes a transcript by a graded
  function of anchor rank, not by a count.
- **Step after rank 2** (U only). Rank 3 behaves like the bottom, so the audit
  counts anchors at ranks 1–2.
- **Step after rank 3** (L only). Rank 3 behaves like the top, so the audit
  counts anchors at ranks 1–3.
- **Unresolved** (neither). n=1,000 does not place rank 3, and the audit's
  summary stays open.

Prediction: graded. In the limit, rejections are nested across ranks, and the
rank 2−3 and rank 3−10 differences come to about 25 and 23 discordant draws.

## Secondary, reported but not deciding

1. Rank 1 against rank 2, two-sided exact McNemar. The limit predicts no
   difference.
2. Naive type-I per rank, with Wilson 95% intervals, a one-sided binomial test
   against 5%, and a KS test; set beside the limit table.
3. Recursive type-I is not significantly above 5% at any rank (Wilson lower
   bound at most 5%).
4. Whether naive point estimates are nonincreasing in rank, and exact McNemar
   tests for rank 5 against 10 and rank 10 against 20.
5. The share of draws with naive p ≤ recursive p, per rank. P4's lemma
   predicts all or nearly all draws at ranks 1 and 2. Its mirror predicts
   the reverse ordering at rank 20.
