# E21: are oblivious searchers calibrated at every menu size and correlation?

Committed before running. Code: `experiments/e21_oblivious_calibration.py`.
This is Table 1 of the note, and the note's gate: if the naive test is not
calibrated when the candidate set is fixed in advance, the note's claim that
*adaptivity* is what breaks it has no control.

## Question

Every result from E15 onward says the realized-menu bootstrap fails when the
search builds candidates from its own results. Table 1 is the other half:
where the candidate set is fixed before any result is seen, the same test
should be exactly calibrated — at any menu size, any trial correlation, and
whether or not the *selection* among those candidates is sequential.

Three claims are under test, each a proposition the note states as known:

- **P1.** An oblivious menu licenses the Reality Check at any trial count and
  any correlation, including near-duplicate trials.
- **P2.** Sub-maximal selection is conservative, never liberal.
- **§4's separation.** Oblivious *generation* with adaptive *selection*
  (LatticeAdaptive) stays calibrated: it is candidate generation, not
  selection, that breaks the test.

## Design

| searcher | menu | oblivious? | selection |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Honest` | 1 pre-registered specification | yes | none |
| `Greedy` | all K singles | yes | best single |
| `GridSearch(subset_sizes=(1,2,3))` | the full lattice | yes | best of the lattice |
| `LatticeAdaptive(max_features=3)` | the full lattice | yes | sequential greedy |

- **Menu size axis is K**, not `max_trials`: subsets up to size 3 saturate the
  cap, so K ∈ {10, 20, 30, 40} gives N = 175, 1,350, 4,525 and 10,700 logged
  columns (measured before writing this). The note's Table 1 carries this as
  its caption: *N is driven by K; lattice size and correlation exposure move
  together.* The two axes are not independent, and the table should not be
  read as separating them.
- **Correlation axis** ω ∈ {0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9}.
- M=50, T=500, T_oos=250, s=0 (pure null, so every rejection is a type-I
  error), sigma=1, B=1500, block length from `select_block_length` per draw
  (measured beforehand: 1 almost everywhere, 2–3 occasionally at ω=0.9).
- **n = 1,000 draws per cell**, seeds 90000–90999, shared across cells so that
  searchers are paired within a draw.
- `Honest`, `Greedy` and `GridSearch` run at all 16 (K, ω) cells.
  `LatticeAdaptive` runs at K ∈ {10, 20} only: its menu is GridSearch's, so it
  costs the same per draw while testing a different question, and two menu
  sizes are enough to separate selection from generation.

## Readings

Per cell: the KS statistic and p-value against Uniform(0,1) for the naive
p-values, and type-I at α = 0.05 with a Wilson 95% interval.

At n = 1,000 the one-sided exact binomial against 5% rejects at 63/1000
(6.3%) and has 80% power at a true rate of 7.0%; the two-sided test detects a
departure below 5% at 37/1000 (3.7%) or fewer.

## Decision rules

There are 56 cells (3 searchers × 16, plus LatticeAdaptive × 8), so roughly
three KS tests would reject at 0.05 by chance alone. Multiplicity is handled
by **Holm's step-down across all cells**, fixed now, not by reading raw
p-values cell by cell.

1. **Gate (primary).** No cell's KS test rejects uniformity after Holm
   correction at 0.05, and every cell's type-I Wilson interval contains 5%.
   - **Holds:** Table 1 goes in the note as the control, and the note's claim
     that adaptive *generation* is the cause stands on a measured contrast
     rather than an assumption.
   - **Fails in any oblivious cell:** the note does not go out. A naive test
     that is miscalibrated with a fixed menu would undercut every comparison
     from §4 onward, and the failure is investigated before anything else.
2. **P2's direction.** `Honest` (N = 1, no selection) and `Greedy` (selection
   over singles) are not liberal: their type-I upper Wilson bounds are
   compatible with 5% or below. A liberal `Honest` cell would indicate a
   problem in the bootstrap itself, not in search.
3. **Selection versus generation.** `LatticeAdaptive`'s type-I is not
   significantly above `GridSearch`'s at the same (K, ω), by exact McNemar on
   the paired draws. Both are oblivious menus; only the selection rule
   differs, so a difference here would contradict SCOPE.md §4.

## Secondary, reported but not deciding

1. Type-I and KS by K at fixed ω, and by ω at fixed K, to show the surface is
   flat in both rather than flat on average.
2. Mean effective breadth per cell (the participation ratio), to record how
   much duplication the ω = 0.9 cells actually carry.
3. The block lengths chosen, and how often the Sharpe guards bind.
4. `Honest`'s deflated Sharpe, which should centre on zero: the sanity check
   that the estimator is not deflating a single pre-registered specification.

## Cost

Measured per draw at ω = 0.3 for a single lattice searcher: 0.19 s (K=10),
1.69 s (K=20), 6.00 s (K=30) and 15.63 s (K=40); `Honest` and `Greedy` are
negligible.

Measured end to end instead, from a 4-draw smoke of the whole grid: 479
CPU-seconds for 224 draw-cells, about 2.5 CPU-s per draw-cell. At n = 1,000
that is roughly **33 CPU-hours**, a little over an hour on 32 cores. (A
component estimate gave 26 CPU-hours; the end-to-end figure is the one to
trust, as it was for E20.) The K = 40 cells hold a 500 × 10,700 return matrix
per draw, which is the memory-heavy corner, so this runs on EC2 rather than
locally.
