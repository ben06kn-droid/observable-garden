# E19(c): does naive inflation grow with the number of candidate features?

Committed before running. Code: `experiments/e19c_feature_count.py`. E19's
other arms, P4 outside exchangeability and the neighbor rule under
heterogeneous correlation, are registered separately.

## Question

Wide datasets offer a search many candidate features. For a search that
builds on its own winner, does the naive bootstrap's distortion grow with
that number? If so, a Reality Check or deflated Sharpe ratio applied to an
adaptive search's log is least reliable exactly where datasets are widest.

## Limit prediction, computed before running

This uses THEORY.md P4's setting: equal-variance, equicorrelated base columns
and the Gaussian limit. The search is winner-anchored at depth 2. It submits
`max(Z_(1), g(Z_(1), Z_(2)))`, with `g(x, y) = (x + y) / √(2 + 2ρ)`, and the
naive null freezes the anchor's index. Monte Carlo of naive type-I,
10⁶ draws per cell:

| feature correlation ρ | K=10 | K=20 | K=40 | K=80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 10.1% | 15.5% | 24.2% | 35.7% |
| 0.3 | 7.3% | 9.1% | 11.3% | 13.7% |

The search's value follows the top two order statistics, whose lead over a
typical feature grows with K. The frozen menu pairs a feature that is
ordinary in each resample. Correlation shrinks the spread of the
feature-specific part, so the effect is weaker at ρ=0.3. At K=20 and ρ=0.3
the winner rule has measured below the limit at finite T: 8.2%, 10.2% and
7.0% in e15, E16 and E17, against 9.1%.

## Design

- `WinnerAnchor(max_features=2)` (`searchers/dose_response.py`).
- K ∈ {10, 20, 40, 80}, nested. Each draw generates 80 features, and the
  search at K sees the first K of them: every data-generator array is sliced
  to its first K features. With s=0 the returns do not depend on the
  features, and a sub-block of an equicorrelated matrix is equicorrelated, so
  each slice is a valid K-feature draw from the same generator. Draws are
  therefore paired across K.
- ρ ∈ {0, 0.3}. M=50, T=500, s=0, sigma=1, B=1500.
- n=500 draws per ρ, seeds 70000–70499, the same seeds at both values of ρ.
- Per K, three nulls:
  - naive: the logged transcript;
  - recursive: replay;
  - full-class: `full_class_matrix(base, 2)`, with 55, 210, 820 and 3,240
    members.

  The block length comes from `select_block_length` on that K's demeaned
  base columns, and the bootstrap seed is the draw's seed.
- Recorded per draw and K: the three p-values, `sr_sel` and the block length.
  Git state is captured at launch.

## Decision rule

The primary test runs separately at each ρ. It is E17's within-draw
permutation trend test on the naive rejection indicators: one-sided,
increasing in K, level scores 1–4, 10,000 permutations, with a draw rejecting
when its naive p-value is below 0.05.

- **p < 0.01 at a given ρ:** the note claims naive inflation grows with the
  number of candidate features at that correlation.
- **p ≥ 0.01:** the note makes no such claim at that ρ.

Power: under the limit model, 400 simulated experiments of 500 nested draws
detected the predicted trend at p < 0.01 every time, at both ρ. That
calculation leaves out finite-sample noise, which adds discordant draws in
both directions and has lowered measured rates below the limit, so it is
optimistic.

## Secondary, reported but not deciding

1. Naive, recursive and full-class type-I per (ρ, K), with Wilson 95%
   intervals and KS tests, set beside the limit table.
2. Recursive and full-class type-I not significantly above 5% at any (ρ, K):
   Wilson lower bound at most 5%.
3. Full-class conservatism against K. The class grows like K²/2, but in the
   limit this search's value equals the class maximum at every K (P5 at
   d = 2), so the limit predicts no conservatism. Finite-T departures, where
   the full-class p-value exceeds the recursive one (7% of draws at K=20 in
   E17), may grow with K. Reported: that share per K, and the trend test on
   full-class rejections, one-sided decreasing in K.
4. The share of draws with naive p ≤ recursive p per (ρ, K). P4's lemma
   predicts all or nearly all.
5. The average change in naive type-I per doubling of K.
